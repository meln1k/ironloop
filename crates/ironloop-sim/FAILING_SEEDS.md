### Seed 61440: the failsafe landing climbs (`no_flyaway`)

Status: known limit, the failsafe climb bias (README, Known limits); not changed by the failsafe fixes below. The quad had less than 3 s of level flight before a 13 s climb at a steady 3.7 m/s at constant throttle. The leaky speed estimate reads that as level after a few seconds, so hover learning took the climbing throttle for hover (0.506 → 0.611). The hold flies at about the same throttle the quad was climbing at, so it sees no acceleration either, and the landing's 96% of that still climbs. An accelerometer cannot tell a steady climb at constant throttle from a hover. Seed 72534 is the same case.

The quad is climbing at about 3.7 m/s toward a 45 m hover when the link drops at 16.4 s. Through `Failsafe(Landing)` from 17.4 s to 24.6 s it keeps climbing at about 2.3 m/s, from 45 m to 62 m, with the throttle swinging between 0.58 and 0.70, until the link returns. Looks like the landing trim and brake keep cycling instead of taking throttle off while the quad is already climbing hard at link loss. Possibly the clearest case of the `failsafe_descends` failures.

### Seed 290248: false `ClockWentBackwards`, then a disarm (`no_false_clock_alarm`)

Status: fixed in the board's control loop (`firmware/mobula6/src/control.rs`) and the simulator's model of it; regression seed.

A 12.6 ms `FcStall` at 47.653 s overlaps an `ImuDropout` that starts 3.5 ms later. At 47.666 s, as the stall ends, the firmware reports `ClockWentBackwards` with no clock fault, and `Disarmed(ImuFailure)` follows 20 ms later. The only one in 1M seeds. Unchecked guess: events queued during the stall are stepped after one stamped later, so the firmware sees a jump back of over 1 ms. If so, either the board can do the same (a firmware bug) or the simulated task queue orders events differently from the board's.

Cause: the last sample before the dropout was ready at 47.656253 s, during the stall. When the stall ended, the loop stepped the ESC telemetry queued during it (stamped up to 47.6655 s) first, as the board's loop does with its `EVENTS` queue, then that sample, 9.8 ms back. Normally a newer data-ready replaces the waiting one every sample, so the queue first is in order; with the IMU silent it is not. On the board, RC frames queued during a long step or stall would do the same. The loop now steps a waiting sample first when it is older than the queued event (`take_drdy_before`, atomic against EXTI1), and the simulator's task does the same. The `Disarmed(ImuFailure)` is right: the dropout lasts 38 ms, and it now comes 20 ms after the last real sample instead of 30.

### Seeds 49056 and 78109: armed upside down, never stopped (`arm_level`, `crash_stops`, `runaway_stops`)

Status: fixed (firmware and monitor); both are regression seeds. Both quads boot on their side and roll onto their backs during gyro calibration, settling at 156°. A z axis read the wrong way round (`imu-z-flipped`, or the chip upside down) turns that into an apparent 24°, just under the 25° arm limit, so they arm. (541565d was right that such a chip reads upside down, but only for a quad sitting upright.) No firmware can see past its only sensor of gravity, so `arm_level` now judges the tilt as the IMU reads it. Once armed, the frame lay pressed into the ground with a motor at full and nothing turning. Runaway detection only looked for spinning, and crash detection needs 60° of estimated tilt, so nothing stopped it. Runaway detection now also stops a frame that never lifted off and does not turn the way the controller asks (over 2 rad/s) for 1 s. That counts while the throttle is above the airmode threshold, or at any throttle once runaway detection has already disarmed that build since power-up. 78109's last arm sits at idle for 5 s, but a sound quad armed at idle on a slope, or one that came down on a sagging pack while the pilot holds a stick (196612), looks the same, so low throttle alone does not count. The `MotorFault` notices after each arm in 78109 are the ESCs starting (every arm shows them), not a signal.

The IMU is mounted flipped (`imu-z-flipped`, `imu-upside-down`) and the quad sits `on-its-side`. It arms at a true tilt of 156° because the estimate says level. 78109 arms and disarms by runaway several times, then stays armed, crashed at 156°, for 4 s, and 5 s after throttle-up. A wrong IMU orientation is a common build mistake. In 78109 all four motors report `MotorFault` right after each arm, which may be a usable signal.

### Seeds 20111 and 45798: runaway detection slow on miswired builds (`runaway_stops`)

Status: 45798 was a monitor problem, fixed, and is a regression seed. 20111 no longer fails, but only because its flight changed; the delay it showed remains. In 45798 the mistakes cancel in the rate loops: the IMU a quarter turn off (`imu-misaligned`), its y axis flipped, and motors 1 and 4 swapped together leave every roll, pitch and yaw output turning the frame the way it pushes, as the gyro reads it. It climbs to 1.5 m with its PIDs near zero, and only the virtual pilot's corrections, applied to mirrored axes, tip it over; to the firmware that looks like the pilot flipping it. `runaway_stops` now applies only to builds whose rate loop pushes an axis the wrong way (`Scenario::rate_loop_reversed`; 305 of the 96,990 build mistakes in seeds 0–999,999 cancel like this, mostly a flipped z axis with reversed props). 20111 (a real reversed pitch loop) climbs at 2.5 m/s on a fresh pack, and its pitch shows nothing until 0.3 s after liftoff in still air. The `pid_high` rule then needs about 0.45 s more, by which time it has passed 1 m. Its second arm used to be disarmed by the yaw-growth check reacting to its own ground hit, which a hit no longer triggers (see the weak-pack `disarm_justified` seeds 800, 1324, 2882, 3127). That arm now disarms 0.34 s later, on the ground, which changes the pilot's later attempts, so the 1 m case no longer comes up at this seed. Of the 3,355 build-mistake seeds in 4,000–39,999, 20111 was the only one failing before and none fails now, but seeds 29519 and 33223 show the same margin: a version of the yaw fix that started its window 20 ms late pushed both past 1 m. Still open: whether to give the simulated quad a little thrust or balance asymmetry, so a reversed loop shows within about 0.1 s of liftoff as on a real frame, or to move the limit.

20111 (`imu-x-flipped`, `motors-swapped-2-3`): runaway detection fires each time, but one arm stays up 666 ms after throttle-up and reaches 1 m. 45798 (three IMU errors, `motors-swapped-1-4`): 1,151 ms, tumbling until crash detection disarms at 10.8 s. Near the property's limit rather than missed; decide whether the limit or the detector should move.

### Seeds 49852, 70904, 141442, 146260: on its side, never stopped (`runaway_stops`, `crash_stops`)

Status: fixed in firmware; 70904 is a regression seed. These came up in a 200k sweep after the yaw-growth check stopped counting ground hits (see 20111). Each is a build with an IMU axis flipped that tips onto its side on takeoff and then rolls or spins there for 5 s with the throttle up. At f431215 the yaw-growth check stopped them only because it took the first ground hit for growth. The flipped axis makes the attitude estimate tumble (it reads 30–150° while the frame lies at 90°), and the gyro shows the frame turning the way every demand asks. So the wedged-frame rule, the tilt-based crash rules and the main runaway rule all see nothing wrong. The accelerometer does: the ground holds the frame up from more than 50° off its thrust axis, which neither thrust nor drag can do. Runaway detection now also stops a frame that never lifted off, with the throttle up (or after an earlier runaway disarm), that has had that side load for 1 s, bounces of under 0.2 s included. Seeds 0–3,999 fail exactly as before.

### Runaway, crash and arming: open cases in 200k seeds

Status: open. From the sweep of seeds 0–199,999 with the fixes above, on f431215.

- **Climbs past 1 m before runaway detection (`runaway_stops`, 7 seeds):** 52807, 67962, 127659, 131214, 185515, 82048 and 182122. Most are `spin-reversed` or `motors-rotated` (yaw reversed); 82048 is the same reversed-pitch delay as 20111. The first five also fail at f431215. 82048 and 182122 are new: their first disarm now comes about 0.25 s later than at f431215, where the yaw-growth check fired on a ground hit (the yaw loop was sound and slowing the spin). That changes the pilot's later re-arms, and a re-arm climbs past 1 m. They share 20111's question: the limit or the detector.
- **102075 (`runaway_stops`):** a reversed build with a dead motor sits upright at full throttle, unable to lift or turn. A sound quad with a dead motor would look the same.
- **False runaway disarms of sound quads (`disarm_justified`, 27 seeds, down from 79):** 20 are yaw growth after a kick or tumble, 4 are yaw-opposed and 2 are the main runaway rule. Six of them (27334, 119497, 119713, 133443, 148322, 164053) are new. f431215 disarmed those six too, but on the ground hit while the frame was over, which the property counts as justified. Now the yaw-opposed check fires about 0.25 s later, after the frame has righted itself. In three of the four yaw-opposed seeds the yaw rate was already falling, so a rule that needs the rate to hold could fix them. Not tried.
- **Motor dead and inverted (`crash_stops`):** 105845, 121708, 122133, 176871 and 194461. The lying rule needs either a recent impact or a roll-over without a fall, and `tipped_fall` (2.3–16 m/s, from a drifting climb estimate) rules out the second.
- **Armed tilted after a stuck or dropped-out IMU (`arm_level`):** 31832, 36216, 85825, 110039, 112686, 113804, 158507, 160104 and 183368. The estimate is wrong at arming.
- **`pilot_can_arm`:** mostly the tilt blocker while the frame settles after a runaway disarm, which is the intended exception. 73883 and the others newly failing were runaway-disarmed at a different moment; at f431215 a false disarm showed up as `disarm_justified` instead.

### Seed 13490: the failsafe hold drops, touchdown not detected (`failsafe_holds`, `failsafe_lands_softly`, `failsafe_disarms_on_touchdown`)

Status: fixed (regression seed). There were three causes. The hold does not drop: the quad starts it in an 8.4 m/s fall from the inverted hang, and drag slows that fall at 5 m/s² whatever the throttle. The hold flew at 1.15 g of thrust, and the requirement is about thrust, so `failsafe_holds` now judges the vertical acceleration from thrust and gravity and leaves drag out (an oracle bug, behind all 19 `failsafe_holds` failures in seeds 0–99,999). The firmware made the same mistake: its hold takes the speed change it measures for a thrust error, read the drag as 15% too much hover throttle, and the landing then flew at about 83% of the real hover thrust. The hold now corrects the hover throttle only if the throttle at link loss was within 15% of it. Then, with the estimate reading a climb after the fall, the landing trim kept taking throttle off a descent that was speeding up; such a descent now counts as sinking. The missed touchdown followed from the 3.6 m/s hit: clipped at 16 g, the accelerometer lost most of the stop, and the estimate read a 2.4 m/s climb on the ground.

The link drops at 35.3 s right after an inverted hang. In the hold the quad accelerates down at 5.1 m/s², touches down at 3.6 m/s, and sits armed on the ground for over 2 s until the link recovers. Looks like the hover throttle is far off after the maneuver, and touchdown detection misses the hard landing.

### Seed 6763: hardest failsafe touchdown, 4.7 m/s (`failsafe_lands_softly`)

Status: fixed (regression seed). Arming started a fresh `Flight`, which zeroed the vertical speed estimate while the quad fell at 6 m/s, so the landing read a 5.6 m/s climb throughout and never braked. The estimate is now integrated disarmed too (once the gyro is calibrated) and kept across arming.

A stuck IMU disarms the quad at 35.9 s; the pilot re-arms in the air at 36.7 s, the link drops 0.3 s later, and the failsafe lands at 4.7 m/s. The failsafe starts with little learned hover throttle after a mid-air re-arm.

### Control quality: 9 seeds (`rate_tracks`, `angle_tracks`, `settles`)

Status: fixed in the monitor (oracle problems, not the controller); 445159, 34731, and 315882 are regression seeds.

- `rate_tracks` 48969, 136715, 143014, 445159: roll rate off by 38–39% RMS; all have `rc-glitch` or `fc-stall` during a stick script.
- `angle_tracks` 133604, 315882, 725433: stick holds reach 69–74% of the commanded angle; 133604 also fails `estimate_tracks`.
- `settles` 34731, 271939: 0.43–0.50 rad/s hands-off roll and pitch; 34731 toggles Angle and Acro several times within 1 s at 13.7 s.

Likely glitched frames or stalls inside the scoring window, so the excuse windows may need adjusting rather than the controller.

Cause, `rate_tracks` and `settles` (6 seeds): in each, one RC frame that passes the CRC (`rc-glitch`) sets a stick near full for one frame, inside a low-amplitude stick hold or the hands-off window. The firmware follows it as far as the motors allow; the first-order reference does not model that, and the 50-150 ms of response carried most of the RMS error. None had `fc-stall` inside the script. The response to a frame off the script is no longer scored for 100 ms after it (`REQ_OFF_SCRIPT_RESPONSE_US`); 0.1 s of off-script sticks still voids the run.

Cause, `angle_tracks`: 315882 starts its script 3.2 s after an impact saturates the gyro, inside the 4 s in which `estimate_tracks` excuses the estimate (17 deg off here); angle mode tilts as far as that estimate says. `angle_tracks` now shares the saturation window (`Cx::reconverging`). 133604 and 725433 also fail `estimate_tracks`: the estimate itself is dragged off 4-5 s after a saturation (see the `estimate_tracks` section).

### `no_esc_stall` (401 in 1M): the weakened motor stalls

Status: fixed in the monitor and the sim (oracle and sim problems); 4285 and 42067 are regression seeds.

All 10 failures in seeds 0-29999 have `motor-degraded`, and in every one the motor that loses sync is the degraded one (60-74% strength), at idle on a pack at 3.0-3.6 V: a weak motor at 10% idle on a sagging pack spins under the ESC's sync limit. The property promises "no ESC loses sync on a sound motor", but the simulated ESC counts any motor still turning as sound, so the monitor counted these. It now judges each motor's stalls only while that motor is healthy. The idle itself holds: with the fix, seeds 0-29999 have no `no_esc_stall` failure, and the "motor idle 5.5%" mutant is still caught.

Seeds 30000-129999 had one more, 42067, on a sound motor: a 190 ms `MotorDesync` on a 3.12 V pack. The simulated ESC stayed `Running` through the fault, so when it cleared it found the motor stopped and, its start ramp capped by that speed, counted a stall 30 ms later. A real ESC that loses sync starts the motor again; the sim's ESC now does too (a sim problem). The restart keeps a desynced motor out for its 50-250 ms open-loop start, so more desyncs tumble the quad: on seeds 30000-129999 `no_esc_stall` now passes everywhere, while `estimate_tracks` goes 211 -> 230, and 22 of the 29 new ones fail 4.0-5.7 s after the gyro saturates, the open post-saturation pull below.

### `motors_quiet` (491 in 1M): a bent prop without eRPM

Status: fixed: bent props in the firmware (a dynamic notch), corrupted RC frames in the monitor (an oracle problem), and three filter bugs in the firmware that the 4.5% limit now holds; 7861, 73501, 1971 and 26033 are regression seeds.

16 of the 17 failures in seeds 0-29999 have both `bent-prop` and `no-telemetry`, and all sit just over the limit (7.5-8.8% RMS). Without eRPM there is no RPM filter, and the 150 Hz gyro low-pass lets the bent prop's shaft-rate tone through to the motors. With telemetry forced on, the same seeds jitter 0.8-1.8%. Betaflight covers this case with its dynamic (FFT) notch. Options: add a dynamic notch, lower the gyro low-pass when eRPM is missing (more lag), or accept it for bent props without bidirectional DShot. The 17th, 7536, is `rc-glitch` corrupting 25% of frames in a 45 m hover (throttle jumps to 100%), the same corrupted-frame issue as the stick scripts above.

The firmware now has a dynamic notch (`crates/ironloop-fc/src/dyn_notch.rs`), on while the RPM filter is off: a 64-sample sliding DFT of roll and pitch, the strongest bin from 100 Hz to 0.45 of the sample rate if it stands 6 times over the mean, placed between bins from the neighbours' complex values, and a Q 3 notch there. Of the 72 seeds that failed `motors_quiet` in seeds 0-29999 and 30000-129999, 50 pass, including every one with `bent-prop` and `no-telemetry`. All 21 still failing have `rc-glitch`: corrupted frames that pass the CRC jump the sticks inside the steady window, so the motors follow the pilot input, not vibration. The monitor now skips `motors_quiet` for 100 ms after the sim sends such a frame (`REQ_OFF_SCRIPT_RESPONSE_US`, as for the stick scripts); the sim passes the time of the last one in `Step::rc_corrupted_us`, since in a hover there is no script to compare the sticks with. All 22 pass.

On seeds 30000-129999 (against the tree before it): `motors_quiet` 57 -> 21 (37 fixed, 43979 new), every property's total 568 -> 538, and no control-quality property fails. The other counts move by a few seeds each way (66 new failures, 60 fixed): `pilot_can_arm` 13 -> 16, `failsafe_levels` 6 -> 9, `failsafe_disarms_on_touchdown` 2 -> 5, `runaway_stops` 2 -> 5. The ones traced are trajectories that change near a fault, not the notch misbehaving: 66780 and 51797 crash or trip a runaway the same way with and without it, then land level instead of on their side after the disarmed tumble, so the pilot arms again (66780 0.3 s before the estimate snaps level; 51797 a spin-reversed build pinned on the ground at full throttle without turning, which the runaway check does not see).

The limit was 7.5%. With the notch in, no current seed came near it (worst 5.25% in seeds 0-29999, 99.9% under 3.1%), so a 250 Hz D-term or a 500 Hz gyro low-pass no longer failed it. The noisiest seeds had three firmware bugs:

- A lost IMU sample (`event-drop`; on the board, a missed data-ready edge) left a two-period gap. A gap now counts as two periods and the filters get the missing sample as the mean of its neighbours.

- A notch's width came from `sin(w0) / 2Q`, which shrinks toward Nyquist. With the ceiling alone, notches at 0.45-0.48 of the rate were too narrow to follow the eRPM's tone and rang at every retune, and the rate loop oscillated at 160-250 Hz (27033 and 14921 went from under 1% to 3-4%).
